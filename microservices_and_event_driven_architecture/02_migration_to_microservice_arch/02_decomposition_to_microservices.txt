- Decomposition by business
Observe and analyze core capabilities that provide value to business and customers e.g revenue, marketing, customer experience
then dedicate a microservice and team to it
Run a though experiment that describes what the system does and what value each capability provides to a non-technical person e.g
Online store: browse product, view product details, read reviews, add product to cart, ship product, update product inventory
giving the products service, shipping, web app, orders, reviews and inventory services. From the names we know that the services do only one thing
and they are cohesive, loosely coupled. Review user journey to verify coupling as viewing store is fulfilled by web app service

- Decomposition by domain/subdomain
We use the developers point of view and use subdomain defined as a sphere of knowledge, influence or capability. Types are
1 core - key differentiator of business from competitor cannot be outsourced  and business has no value without it
2 Supporting - integral in delivering the core capabilities and is not necessarily different from competitors
3 Generic - Not specific to any business and can be bought off the shelf in many cases
The categorization helps prioritize investment into each subdomain, allocate engineers by experience and save time with existing solution

In an online store the *core* is the products catalog
supporting is the orders, inventory and shipping
Generic are reviews, payment, search, images, image compression, security, web ui

We can dedicate a microservice to each subdomain or group several into one microservice and if it makes sense to separate them we can do that

*** Other methods Decomposition by Action, Decomposition by entity

Decomposition by business vs domain/subdomain

- Cohesion and loose coupling   By business wins
- Size of microservice  By domain wins
- Stability of the design   By business wins
- Intuitive for engineers   By Subdomain wins

There is no one right way to set boundaries what works now may not in the future and they may change as features are added
No perfect decomposition as there will be some coupling and friction
Techniques are guidelines but not bullet proof
