Motivation for Microservices
• Independent Deployability: Teams can update, test, and deploy individual services without rebuilding or releasing the entire application.
• Data and Fault Isolation: Crashing or overloading one service (such as a recommendation engine) does not take down unrelated domains (such as checkout or authentication).
• Targeted Scaling: High-load components can be scaled horizontally and independently, optimizing cloud infrastructure and costs.

Microservices architecture is the most modern and popular style in the industry where a system is organized as a collection of independent services,
each with its own scope of responsibility and fully owned by a dev team. Most successful companies use this architecture and is considered one of the
biggest contributors to their success. When done correctly allows organizations to scale to thousands or tens of thousands engineers divides into small
teams that operate independently, enabling building of systems that serve millions of users while keeping operational cost lows and stay efficient and
innovative.

Challenges
This architecture is beneficial under the right conditions when applied correctly or it will introduce a lot of unnecessary overhead with no benefits.

Motivation for event driven
Commonly used with microservice architecture to achieve even greater decoupling
• Eliminating Synchronous Bottlenecks: Instead of making blocking, chain-reaction REST or RPC calls where a slow downstream dependency halts the entire request, services emit non-blocking notification events.
• Loose Coupling: Producers only publish that an occurrence happened (e.g., OrderPlaced) without knowing or caring which services consume it or how they process it.
• Asynchronous Fan-Out: A single business event can simultaneously trigger multiple independent reactions—such as updating inventory, running fraud detection, and sending a confirmation email—without extra overhead for the sender.
• Resilience and Reliability: Using message brokers like Apache Kafka or RabbitMQ allows events to be buffered if a consumer goes offline, and replayed later to recover state or debug issues.
